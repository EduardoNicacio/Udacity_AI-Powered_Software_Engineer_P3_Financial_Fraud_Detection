# Project Rubric

Use this project rubric to understand and assess the project criteria.

## Understanding Microservices Architecture

### Criteria

At the end of the project, a learner will be able to identify key functions of a system and define microservices to fulfill these.

### Submission Requirements

You must provide discussion on your functional decomposition. At minimum this will include:

- The overall requirements of the solution, broken down into individual microservices that will perform these functional units. There are a minimum of 3 different functions that should be broken out.
- Microservice function, inputs and outputs are detailed
- Any Inter-service communication and coupling is considered and discussed.

### Criteria

At the end of the project, learners should be able to identify key event patterns and use these to drive their microservices pattern.

### Submission Requirements

When defining events and triggers for your services, ensure:

- Key events for the system are identified
  - What, when, and why?
- Publishers and consumers are clearly outlined
- Any architectural decisions that deviate from an event driven pattern are highlighted and explained

You must define a trigger, likely based on an event, for each microservice.

## Designing for Observability

### Criteria

At the end of the project, a learner will be able to analyze and communicate key performance characteristics of their solution.

### Submission Requirements

You must consider the needs of your system, and in doing so, highlight 3 key metrics that need to be monitored.

### Criteria

At the end of the project, a learner will be able to design for rich system telemetry.

### Submission Requirements

System components should be easily observable and expose information in an extensible way that ensures a system continues to be maintainable as it grows.

You must include a section that details the observability of your solution.

As part of any observability considerations, detail:

- How logs and metrics are collected
- How logs, metrics and spans might be correlated

## Designing for Resilience

### Criteria

At the end of the project, a learner will be able to identify key failure modes in their system and provide mitigation strategies for them.

### Submission Requirements

In scalability and resilience considerations, you must note clearly the following items:

- Any single points of failure in your solution
- How these have, or could be, mitigated
- How the solution handles the possibility of data loss in these scenarios, and how you might restore anything that is lost

### Criteria

At the end of the project, a learner will be able to communicate strategies for scaling a system to meet demand.

### Submission Requirements

As part of your selection of technologies and decomposition of functionality, you must indicate how these components of your solution can be scaled.

You need to consider and detail how the following can be scaled:

- Individual microservices
- Analytic workloads
- Event messaging

## Communicating Architectures

### Criteria

At the end of the project, a learner is able to communicate high level solutions to complex requirements

### Submission Requirements

Your solution must cover key criteria without getting lost in the technical detail. Across the report as a whole, you need to:

- Identify the core problems to be solved. You are moving from business problems to technical problems, and while the business problems are important, they should be decomposed to technical objectives in order to provide a clear solution.
- Provide a component-level outline of how these problems will be solved. Ensure that you highlight what each component achieves.
- Discuss the rationale as to why certain approaches have been taken. Highlight patterns that you have used, and what the cost-benefit of using these patterns is.
- Provide high-level guidance. The purpose of the document is not to prescribe the exact approach for what the content of messages should be, or how individual microservices should be written, only to provide patterns and guidance on how the overall solution should be structured.

### Criteria

At the end of the project, a learner will be able to compose meaningful and maintainable architecture diagrams.

### Submission Requirements

Your report must contain a useful amount of diagrams.

At a minimum, you must have:

- Solution Architecture Diagram showing interactions between components
- Event flow diagram showcasing each event type
- End-to-end transaction flow diagram

You may have additional diagrams as appropriate. In general, provided you are not repeating any content that has already been represented, it is good to have a graphical representation.

## Suggestions to Make Your Project Stand Out

Suggest some ways that this might be deployed in reality. What sort of compute provider might underpin this? How would you need to adapt any of your design in order to conform to idiosyncrasies in that system?

Production systems are usually designed with an expected range of usage profiles and purposes. However, these can, and often do, fail to capture the full breadth of usage. For instance, our system will likely experience much heavier than normal load during compliance audits and end-of-financial-year activities.

What can be done **proactively** to ensure it remains operable during these events?
