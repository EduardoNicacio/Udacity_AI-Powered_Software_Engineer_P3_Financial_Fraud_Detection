# Project Instructions

## Deliverable

Using your understanding of common architectural patterns, you will formulate a solution architecture report for a robust, scalable, and observable system capable of meeting the key criteria outlined in the project overview. This report may contain:

- Detailed architectural diagrams illustrating the system's components and their interactions.

- Data flow diagrams demonstrating the journey of a transaction through the system.

- A proposed technology stack with clear justifications for each selection.

- A detailed plan for implementing distributed tracing, centralized logging, and metrics, including specific tools and data points.

- A breakdown of how any Service Mesh, BFF, caching, and event-driven patterns contribute to the system's scalability, resilience, security, and observability.

- A critical evaluation and discussion of the trade-offs and challenges inherent in their chosen design, particularly in a financial services context.

## Steps

**Understand the Problem & Requirements:**
Analyse the problem statement and understand the key requirements laid out.

**Define Core Microservices:**
Identify and logically group the key functionalities into distinct, loosely coupled microservices. Note the primary responsibility of these microservices.

When composing your solutions individual units of work, ensure:

- Overall requirements of the solution are broken down into individual microservices that will perform these functional units.
- Microservice function, inputs and outputs are considered
- Inter-service communication and coupling is considered and discussed.

**Design Event-Driven Communication:**
Think about how these microservices will communicate between each other. Define these events and flows using a messaging mechanism, and propose schemas for the events.

When defining events and triggers for your services, ensure:

- Key events for the system are identified
  - What, when, and why?
- Publishers and consumers are clearly outlined
- Any architectural decisions that deviate from an event driven pattern are highlighted and explained

**Architect the Frontend:**
Design the frontend for the project, specifically around how it will fetch data while remaining responsive. Consider how performance can be optimised.

**Observability:**
Observability concerns should form part of your report. Develop a solution for aggregating logs, traces and metrics across this solution. Identify some key metrics that we may want to use for alerting. In particular, you should consider what metrics are important for a system like this, and define some reasonable SLOs.

From this you should be able to narrow down the specific metrics you need to instrument in order to measure whether or not you are meeting these objectives.

Your SLOs should be specific, measurable, and objective. As an example:

- "99% System uptime" - bad SLO
- "99% of GET calls to the frontend service are completed in less than 200ms" - Good SLO

**Suggest stack:**
Select appropriate technologies (databases, frameworks, infrastructure components) for each architectural layer and microservice. Provide clear justifications for these choices based on architectural requirements and trade-offs.

**Bring it all together:**
Consolidate all the previously made decisions into a comprehensive architectural document.

Throughout your report you need to provide visual aids to detail your architecture. As mentioned in the instructions and environment setup, these can be done in any of the approved formats.

When describing relationships between components or entities, you must provide a diagram. The diagram must clearly illustrate the relationship, whether this is a data flow, an event or otherwise.

Clear diagrams lay the foundation for your architecture to be well understood, so ensure that they are provided liberally throughout your report.

**Critically Evaluate:**
Analyze and articulate any significant architectural trade-offs made and why these are worthwhile.

Ensure that you discuss the rationale as to why certain approaches have been taken. Highlight patterns that you have used, and what the cost-benefit of using these patterns is, particularly around risk in the financial services domain.

## Submission Instructions

The report may be developed in word processors, or IDEs, but ultimately must be submitted as a PDF file. This is required for readability across devices.
